# Volume Profile & Liquidity Data: Knowing the Capital Flows

For our system, price alone is not enough. A robust analysis must imperatively integrate knowledge of **capital flows** and **market liquidity**. This is why we use Volume Profile and derivatives data as essential filters to validate the relevance of a harmonic pattern.

## The Volume Profile: Identifying High Accumulation Zones

The **Volume Profile** is an advanced analysis tool that displays the total volume traded at each price level over a given period. Unlike traditional volume (which is based on time), the Volume Profile shows us "where" the activity took place.

We use three key data points from the Volume Profile to validate our signals:

-   **Point of Control (PoC):** The price level where the largest volume was traded. It is the "fair price" perceived by the market and acts as a powerful magnet or rejection zone.
-   **Value Area High (VAH):** The highest price level of the "value area" (where ~70% of the volume took place).
-   **Value Area Low (VAL):** The lowest price level of the "value area."

A harmonic pattern whose entry point (D point) coincides with one of these three levels has a much higher probability of success. This means that the expected reversal occurs in an area where the market has already shown significant interest, making the level harder to break.

## Derivatives Data: Measuring Systemic Weakness (Exotic Alpha)

Derivatives data (such as futures and options) give us insight into the **speculation** and **positioning of market players**. We integrate this data as **Exotic Alpha** to measure the "systemic weakness" of the market, a crucial filtering factor for our Confidence Score.

The key metrics we analyze are:

-   **Liquidations:** When leveraged positions are forcibly closed, it creates sudden buying or selling pressure. We monitor the price levels where large liquidation pools are concentrated. A pattern that forms just before one of these levels has a greater chance of being "pushed" by the cascade of liquidations.
-   **Open Interest (OI):** The total open interest in derivatives contracts. A sharp increase in OI in a trend can signal over-leverage and potential exhaustion.

By integrating this data, our system does not just find a beautiful geometric "shape"; it ensures that the context of capital flows and liquidity is also favorable. This is an essential step to avoid market "traps" where a pattern looks perfect but fails due to unfavorable underlying market conditions.
